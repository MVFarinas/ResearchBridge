# Scientific Paper Accessibility App – Project Notes

## Core Premise

Knowledge is no longer centralized to educational institutions such as universities and libraries. Large amounts of scientific and academic knowledge are now available online through platforms such as:

- Google Scholar
- Scientific publishing journals
- Open-access repositories
- Research databases

However, despite the availability of information, a major barrier still exists:

> Academic language itself.

The wording, writing style, assumptions of prior knowledge, and highly specialized terminology used in academic papers often prevent everyday readers from fully understanding the material.

This creates several downstream problems:

- People avoid reading papers in full
- Readers cherry-pick conclusions or isolated statements
- Important limitations and nuances are missed
- Misinterpretations spread easily
- Scientific knowledge remains inaccessible to the general public

The goal of this project is to remove that final barrier and make scientific knowledge understandable and accessible to everyone.

---

# Project Vision

Create an application that helps users:

- Read scientific papers in plain English
- Understand difficult concepts through contextual explanations
- Explore the significance and limitations of research
- Evaluate the credibility and impact of papers
- Engage in community discussion around research

The application should preserve the integrity of the original paper while making it easier for non-experts to understand.

---

# Core Features

## 1. Plain-English Paper Rewriting

The application should rewrite academic papers in a more understandable and accessible format.

### Key Principles

- Not merely paraphrasing
- Not oversimplifying to the point of losing meaning
- Preserve the original intent and scientific accuracy
- Add context where necessary

### Contextualization

When a paper references complex concepts or assumed background knowledge:

- The rewritten version should provide brief contextual explanations
- Add approximately 1–2 explanatory sentences when needed
- Avoid excessively bloating the text
- Keep explanations concise but understandable

### Goal

Allow readers with little or no prior domain knowledge to follow the paper meaningfully.

---

## 2. Original Paper + Inline Explanations

The original paper should remain available in its untouched form.

### Concept Explanation System

Readers should be able to interact with difficult terms or passages to receive explanations.

Possible approaches discussed:

### Option A — Community Annotation System

Inspired by the music annotation platform Genius.

Users could:

- Annotate phrases or passages
- Explain terminology in simpler language
- Provide additional context
- Help others understand difficult concepts

### Option B — LLM Annotation System

Instead of user-created annotations, annotations could be generated by an LLM.

Potential capabilities:

- Automatic concept explanations
- Context-aware simplifications
- Scientific term breakdowns
- Dynamic educational support

This feature is still under consideration and may replace or coexist with community annotations.

---

## 3. Research Summary Engine

The application should generate a high-quality summary that goes beyond the paper abstract.

### Problem with Existing Abstracts

Abstracts and conclusions often:

- Omit important nuance
- Do not fully explain limitations
- Can be misleading when read alone
- May not communicate statistical significance clearly

### Desired Summary Output

The app should generate concise but detailed summaries covering:

- Main findings
- Implications
- Statistical significance
- Limitations
- Conflicts of interest
- Definitive takeaways
- Practical relevance

The goal is to help readers understand what the paper actually says — not merely what they assume it says.

---

## 4. Credibility / Impact Metrics

The app should help users evaluate the legitimacy and importance of papers.

### Potential Metrics

#### Citation Index

- Number of citations
- Citation growth over time
- Influence within the field

#### Journal Prestige / Publisher Quality

Examples discussed:

- Higher trust in papers from respected journals (e.g., Nature)
- Lower trust in predatory journals or paper mills

#### Recency

- More recent research may be more relevant in fast-moving domains

#### Readability / Convolution Score

A score indicating:

- How convoluted or inaccessible the writing is
- How difficult the paper is for general audiences

#### Preliminary Knowledge Burden Score

Measure how much prior knowledge is assumed by the paper.

Potential metric:

- How well the paper explains foundational concepts itself
- How much external knowledge the reader must already possess

---

## 5. Discussion Forum / Community Thread

Each paper could include a discussion section underneath.

Users could:

- Ask questions
- Discuss interpretations
- Debate findings
- Share clarifications
- Engage with others reading the same paper

This discussion component is considered important even if community annotations are not included.

# Design Philosophy

## Preserve Scientific Integrity

The project should avoid:

- Misrepresenting scientific findings
- Over-simplifying complex research
- Turning nuanced research into clickbait summaries

Instead, the focus is on:

- Accessibility
- Context
- Transparency
- Scientific literacy

# Target Audience

Potential users include:

- General public
- Students
- Interdisciplinary researchers
- Journalists
- Professionals outside a given field
- Curious learners
- People trying to verify scientific claims online

---

# Core Problem Statement

Scientific knowledge is technically available to everyone, but practically understandable to very few.

This application aims to bridge the gap between:

- Scientific research
  and
- Public understanding

by translating, contextualizing, and organizing research in a way that preserves accuracy while improving accessibility.

---

# Potential Long-Term Impact

If successful, the platform could:

- Improve scientific literacy
- Reduce misinformation caused by cherry-picking research
- Help users critically evaluate studies
- Encourage deeper engagement with research papers
- Democratize access to scientific understanding
